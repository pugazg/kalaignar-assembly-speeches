**Speech: 7**  
**Date: 14.05.1998**

**Hon. Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Hon. Speaker, on the basis of the views expressed by the Hon. members on the demand for grants for the Industries Department, and on the basis of the state of industrial growth in Tamil Nadu, I propose to place a few views here and also make some important announcements.

The Hon. member Thiru Ranganathan, who opened the debate, asked whether all the industrial projects announced so far had been implemented, thereby creating the necessity for me to place that list before the House. Therefore, I shall explain that in my speech.

Our Hon. member Thiru Subbarayan pointed out that power generation is fundamentally of great importance for industrial growth, and he too raised the questions of what efforts this Government has undertaken for that purpose, whether we have generated enough electricity to meet demand, and why we have not done so.

Many members have referred here to factories needed in their respective constituencies and districts. If I mention only one or two of them here, it does not mean that the requests of the other members have all been ignored.

### Source page 200

In particular, the leader of our Indian National League, Thiru Latheef, has for many years been struggling here, with distress, asking for a sandalwood factory. At least in order somehow to fulfil his wish, whether it brings a loss or a profit, an effort will be made this year to start it, and I convey that to him. (Sound of desk-thumping.)

The leader of our All India Forward Bloc party, Thiru Santhanam, has made the request that coconuts are produced in large quantities in his constituency and that a coconut industry should be started there. I inform him that the Government will consider that matter and take appropriate action.

Dr. Krishnasamy, the Legislative Assembly member for our Ottapidaram constituency, has asked for a spinning mill. After examining the advantages, disadvantages and feasibility of locating a spinning mill in that constituency, the consequences that may arise from it, the profit that may be obtained, the employment that may benefit people, and the number of people who may obtain that employment, without insisting that it must necessarily be a spinning mill, this Government will come forward to create some industry that can provide employment in that dry region, which I wish to inform the House.

The Marxist Communist Party Legislative Assembly member Mani has frequently referred in this House to a rubber factory. Last year I too gave an assurance on it, saying that I myself would take the lead and involve myself in that effort. Fortunately, he did not point that out here. But I remember it. I wish to explain it to him. For starting a rubber industrial estate in Kanyakumari district, the consultancy work has been entrusted to the organisation called the ‘Rubber Board, Kottayam’. They have been given authority to conduct that study.

### Source page 201

The Rubber Board’s report will be received within three months. On that basis, it will first be decided how much should be done, and immediate further action will be taken, which I convey to them.

Thiru Mani, member of Pattali Makkal Katchi 1, said that some industrial complex should be established at Pennagaram. After I present a list showing how all the industrial complexes established in Tamil Nadu are functioning, I shall, as a continuation of that, examine whether there is a possibility of establishing an industrial complex there and inform Thiru Mani here that the Government will take a decision accordingly.

Thiru Raman, who spoke last, and Thiru Ranganathan, who spoke at the beginning, referred here to the Arakkonam plant. They pointed out the condition in which workers there have lost their jobs and are living. It is not that this Government is without concern about that. But I consider it proper that the members of this House should know some details about it.

The Tamil Nadu Steel Corporation at Arakkonam was established in 1972 as a public-sector undertaking. It was this Government that established it. The undertaking functioned in two sections. One was the iron-melting workshop — Steel Melting Shop — and the other was the electric rolling mill — Re-rolling Mill. This plant, which had operated profitably in its initial stages, later reached a very distressed condition. The reason for this was obsolete and non-functioning machinery.

### Source page 202

In the intervening period, all these became obsolete and non-functioning machines.

Not only that, the prices of raw materials required for production increased. Maintenance was not properly carried out in the intervening period; another factor was that the production cost became higher than the selling price. All these became reasons for the plight of this plant. The plant continued to operate only at a loss. The loss in 1994-95 was Rs. 1.88 crore; in 1995-96, Rs. 5.91 crore; in 1996-97, Rs. 11.78 crore. The accounts for 1997-98 have not yet been finalised. Therefore, up to 1996-97 the total loss was Rs. 19.57 crore.

Since the Government itself fixes the price of the products manufactured by the Tamil Nadu Steel Corporation — administered price — the Government also has to provide as subsidy the difference between the production cost and the price at which they are sold. The Government itself has to provide a subsidy to make up that intervening amount between the production cost and the selling price. If the Rs. 53 crore provided so far in this manner is added, the total loss comes to Rs. 72 crore and 57 lakh.

From 25-6-1997, one part of the Steel Melting Shop’s operations was stopped, and when the management told the employees to work in the electric rolling mill — that is, in the Twisting work, there is another section, is there not? the section doing Twisting work — the employees refused to do that work.

### Source page 203

As a consequence, the electric rolling mill too has not operated since 18-8-1997. The expert committee constituted to rehabilitate this plant expressed the view that the assets belonging to the Steel Corporation could be sold and all its debts settled.

That is, the expert committee suggested that the Steel Corporation’s assets could be sold and only this Twisting work could be carried on. Since the amount receivable by the Tamil Nadu Steel Corporation was only Rs. 5.75 crore, while the debt payable by the undertaking was Rs. 40 crore, that too was not workable. In this situation, because the efforts undertaken to rehabilitate the plant did not yield results and the workers also did not cooperate, the Government decided to close the plant. Even so, on the ground that the workers should not be left to suffer, just as an agreement had been reached in the B and C Mill, on the basis that workers who wished to leave could do so, the Arakkonam steel-plant workers were given more than what had been given to the B and C Mill workers. Rs. 1.25 lakh plus gratuity — concessions of this kind were all provided to them. Nearly 90 per cent of them agreed to it, obtained the applications, returned the applications, and declared that they agreed to it.

Similarly, when it was proposed to implement this voluntary retirement scheme there, the workers have been asking that they should be given more than what was given here in the B and C Mill. We have agreed to give that too. If it is Rs. 1.25 lakh here, we have agreed to give them more than that, to an extent that would satisfy them. But they are expecting a very, very high amount. I inform the House that the Government is considering this because, if this is fulfilled today, a situation may arise in which workers in other factories subsequently make a similar demand.
