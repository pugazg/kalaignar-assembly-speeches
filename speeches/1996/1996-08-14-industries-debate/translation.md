They have also constructively pointed out the work that still has to be done in the industrial sector and, through their concern that progress should be achieved in this field, have given further encouragement to this Government, which is undertaking efforts with determination. I convey my thanks to all of them.

The previous Government came to an end after the recently concluded general election, and this Government has now been in office for roughly 95 days. In these 95 days, the distinguished members of this House, who have clearly understood how many progressive measures have taken place in every sector in Tamil Nadu, have offered both their appreciation and advice on how this Government should conduct itself so that it may deserve still greater appreciation.

As far as industrial policy is concerned, in Tamil Nadu there has often been a rise and fall, followed by correction, then another decline and another correction, and such a situation has been unavoidable.

When it is said that Tamil Nadu achieved great industrial growth during Congress rule, large funds were allocated by the Central Government for the First Five-Year Plan, Second Five-Year Plan and Third Five-Year Plan, and Tamil Nadu obtained the benefit that enabled it to gain industrial development. But when the Fourth and Fifth Five-Year Plans began, adequate funds were not allocated for those plans. As some Hon. members here have pointed out, Tamil Nadu could not obtain funding on the scale received by certain northern States; not only that, it could not obtain allocations that would keep it in the leading position, and industrial growth declined. In particular, nobody can deny that there has been a major setback in the industrial sector during the last five years.

The reason is that industrialists, entrepreneurs and industrial institutions wishing to start industries found even entering Tamil Nadu a difficult matter. As Hon. member Ranganathan pointed out, there prevailed in Tamil Nadu an unhealthy, unacceptable and regrettable situation in which those coming to start an industry had to pay even to meet the appropriate persons. Only in these past two or three months have we in the Government been openly meeting and speaking with industrialists. As stated in the Budget and as I pointed out in the reply during that debate, industrialists have now understood that, with regard to industries, industrial projects and negotiations with industrialists, the Kazhagam Government is an open book.
