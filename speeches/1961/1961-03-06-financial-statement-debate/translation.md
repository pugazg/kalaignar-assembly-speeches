## Kalaignar's Legislative Assembly Speeches on the Financial Statement

**Speech: 4**  
**Date: 6.3.1961**

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Honourable Speaker, I rise to state here my views on the financial statement for 1961-62. The Honourable Minister who first read this financial statement pointed out with great pride that, at the beginning of the statement, it is described as the “Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.” But an English translation of this financial statement has been given to us. When we read that statement, there is no way for us to rejoice at what we see. I wish to point out here that it causes pain. I cannot refrain from pointing out that the happiness we feel on seeing “Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly” written in the statement is only the happiness of having crossed half a well. The scholar Dr. Jeyarson, an expert with great command of good language, has written very beautifully about this. He has written that changing the name of a town, a country or a capital is not a matter of removing one fence and putting up another. In the same way, when changing the name of a country in accordance with the wishes of the people who live in that country, his golden words should be kept in mind. I wish only to point out that we cannot become happy merely because it has been changed and written as “Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.”

When the Honourable Minister read this statement, he read it with great pride. He read it proudly on the ground that there is no tax of any kind in this statement. But all the people of Tamil Nadu are suffering, unable to bear the taxes imposed by the Central Government. We must not forget to ask whether they will take pride in seeing that. The people here are unable to enjoy the pride that no tax has been imposed here, because the cruelty of the Central Government's taxation is tormenting them. I therefore wish to say that a situation has arisen in which the Ministers here cannot fully enjoy the pride that there is no tax here. Only if they free themselves from the bonds of the Central Government can they too fully enjoy that pride, and on their behalf we too can certainly enjoy that pride fully.

For, when the statement speaks about the food problem, the Minister expresses confidence that food production has made very considerable progress this year and that, within the Third Plan period, the target of an additional 15 lakh tons of food production can be expected. At the same time, he gives the distressing news that chemical fertiliser stands in the way of reaching that Plan target: in 1957 we received only 1.2 lakh tons, and in 1961 we will receive only about 1.3 lakh tons. He also states that unless irrigation facilities increase, it will be difficult to reach the Plan target. Even if one tries to escape by saying that the obstacle to reaching the target in foodgrain production is the shortage of irrigation—

—facilities, a friend who spoke before me said with pride that his constituency was rich in water and fertile land, and that such resources could be seen throughout Tamil Nadu. But, specifically and particularly, I have repeatedly said in this Assembly that the Kudaganaru scheme in the Kulithalai constituency, from which I have been elected, must be taken up. That scheme, which could provide irrigation to more than 7,050 acres, has been estimated to cost Rs. 20 lakh and has been included in the Third Plan. When even there it has been placed eighth in the (B)1 category, the doubt arises whether it has merely been included as an eyewash: will this scheme be implemented, and will this Government take the necessary steps for it?

The reason why even small irrigation schemes of this kind—schemes costing Rs. 20 lakh or Rs. 10 lakh—are not being implemented is that the share we asked for, the share due to Tamil Nadu, has not been given. Even from what we asked for, Rs. 100 crore is cut and we are brushed aside. That is why even small schemes such as Kudaganaru are left unimplemented. And now, in the pitiable situation in which, from this year, they are to lose even the amount that had been given to help meet the difficulty caused by increasing the salaries of their employees, I request the Finance Minister to consider why we have been reduced to throwing up our hands and saying: we asked, we begged, we pleaded, we even knelt down, but there was no use.

Even if food production is not increased, prices continue to rise day by day. What have they done to control them? I wish to refer to a Bill passed by the Ceylon Parliament. In order to prevent the prices of essential commodities and foodstuffs there from continuing to rise, and to keep them within a certain limit, the Ceylon Parliament has passed a Bill. That Bill provides for severe action against any trader who commits an offence by violating the law. We see this in the newspapers. Can this Government bring forward such a Bill? I know that this Government has neither the strength nor the means to bring forward such a Bill. At this point I wish specifically to ask why they should not place the matter before the Central Government and try to control prices.

It may be asked how such a Bill can be brought in within so large an empire. That is precisely why I say this: there is no need for it to be such a large empire. It is enough even if it is a small country, like Ceylon. I wish to state that only if Tamil Nadu functions separately, only if Dravida Nadu exists as a separate State, can the great Tamil people of this land be protected.

In 1952, the price of second-grade rice had risen by 7%. I must state with regret that in 1960 it had risen by 73%. Charcoal 25%, firewood 38%, textiles 31%—when the price of every commodity is rising like this, if it is left uncontrolled while food production is said to be increasing, it is like a horse growing fat without a bridle, an ox growing fat—

—without a nose-rope. If things are simply allowed to continue in that fashion, the country will deteriorate. I therefore wish to say that the Central Government must be pressed immediately to control prices.

At a time when food production is being spoken of in grand terms, one Minister announced that crops in Thanjavur district had been damaged on a large scale by cyclone and heavy rain, while another Minister announced that they had not been damaged. Even after it has become clear, at this harvest time, that the paddy grains have all turned into chaff, this Government has not developed the good sense to grant tax remission to those lands. I state this with regret in this debate on the financial statement.

On page 97 of the Third Plan draft it is stated that the Tuticorin Harbour Expansion Scheme is included in the Sethusamudram Project. But the financial statement says that the Sethusamudram Project is linked with the development of Tuticorin Harbour. It is also said that the scheme for developing Tuticorin Harbour will be pressed for inclusion in the Third Plan. If matters proceed like this, there will be neither Tuticorin nor Sethusamudram. When we ask about these matters, they speak of North and South and say it is wrong to speak in this way. Sri Kakkan says, “All you know is partition of the country.” The Finance Minister too has taken part in the all-party meetings at which Mr. Anna and others speak. Has he not understood the matters discussed there? When we emphasise democracy—

—they ask whether we know what democracy is, call this ignorance, and point out that our party has become a laughing-stock at every street corner. Do the people not know whose party is the laughing-stock? That will not make us a laughing-stock. Yesterday, from Andhra to Delhi, their party became a laughing-stock. The Mysore matter is a laughing-stock; Ministers taking officials along to collect election funds is a laughing-stock. All these things must be recognised and considered.

**Mrs. T. N. Ananthanayaki:** Sir, on a point of order.
The Hon. Member can speak only about the Budget and this House is not a Party platform to conduct any propaganda. I am not able to understand the Hon. Member referring to "Santhi Siruppu" and I want a ruling from the Chair whether it is parliamentary.

**Temporary Chairman Mr. S. M. Annamalai:** That is not a point of order.

**A Member:** It is not a point of order.

**Mrs. T. N. Ananthanayaki:** Only the Chair can say that and not the Hon. Member.

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** I know what I should speak about in a Budget speech. Thinking of the fact that the lady does not know it, I regret her ignorance, to use the Minister's language.
