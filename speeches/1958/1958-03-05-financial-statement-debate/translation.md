## Kalaignar's Legislative Assembly Speeches on the Financial Statement

**Speech: 1**  
**Date: 5.3.1958**

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Honourable Speaker, I am duty-bound to place before you my views on the financial statement submitted to this House by the Finance Minister.

Beginning with the praise of rain, “It gives food to those who eat, and itself becomes food to those who drink,” the statement explains that, if we are to live prosperously, the North must bestow its boon upon us; and it ends on the sad full stop that one among those whom we trusted, one who was described as one among us, has returned. If one reads and rereads that statement of nearly 26 pages...

**Hon. C. Subramaniam:** Never mind. At least you have looked at the number of pages in the Budget. That itself is progress!

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Not merely the pages; I also wish to point out the absurdity in the statistics given in it—

<!-- source-page: 19 -->

When I probed and probed to see what difference there is between last year's statement and this year's statement, I found on page 5 that the statistics say, in the English statement they have given, that we had planned to supply 140,000 tons of ammonium sulphate fertiliser, while in the Tamil statement they say we had planned one lakh and four thousand tons. Apart from a few discrepancies such as that error, there is no difference at all between that statement and this one. We went to the Central Government and asked for assistance. Those requests for assistance were ignored. They were fulfilled only to a limited extent. I cannot refrain from pointing out that the same conditions seen in last year's statement are found in this year's statement as well.

Some taxes have been imposed in this year's statement. No taxes were imposed in last year's statement. Honourable members of the Congress party had said that those in the Opposition were disappointed because there was nothing about which they could speak. I wish to ask whether those same members have not spoken this year against the taxes that have been imposed. I would like to remind the House of the elegant remark made by the Honourable Finance Minister in his previous Budget speech: there is a difference between the way a cat seizes a mouse and the way the same cat carries its kitten. The Congress members who spoke against the tax have expressed themselves not like a cat carrying its kitten, but like a cat seizing a mouse. I also wish to point that out. This year's statement refers to agricultural income tax. There is opposition to it within the Congress! Likewise, a tax has been imposed on entertainment, and they have spoken against that too. Agricultural income tax—

<!-- source-page: 20 -->

—it is said, is to be imposed this very year. During the previous Ministry, Honourable Members had demanded that agricultural income tax should be imposed. What reply was given then? They said that taxes had already been imposed on coffee and tea estates, and therefore there was no need to impose a tax on agricultural income. They also said that a ceiling was going to be placed on landholdings and that, for that reason too, there was no need to impose such a tax. I now remember that reply. Even in the address of His Excellency the Governor, it is stated that the time when a ceiling will be placed on landholdings is fast approaching. But that ceiling has not come now. Agricultural income tax has come in its place. Entertainment too is being taxed. As far as the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is concerned, they would have expected us to oppose it! They would have expected: “It is in their party that there are people involved in cinema, so the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam will certainly oppose this.” Contrary to what the members may have thought, I wish to say that, if the Government considers this tax necessary, we do not oppose it.

**Hon. C. Subramaniam:** They were the ones who made this suggestion last year. That is why they accept it.

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** I offer our appreciation and thanks for having taken up at least this much of our suggestion.

<!-- source-page: 21 -->

I wish to mention one matter concerning handloom. The previous Council of Ministers accepted that the production of bordered dhotis and saris should be reserved for handloom, and that decision is sleeping in a dirty corner of the Central Government's mansion. This Government has not been able to shake it awake. Nor has this Government yet taken the necessary steps to wake it up and act upon it.

This Government has also failed to make use of many facilities available here. There are many schemes that ought to be undertaken. If we say that they should be taken up and the country's prosperity increased, they say there are no facilities to carry them out. They even say that there are no natural advantages in the South. When I hear this, a rare couplet from the Tirukkural comes to my mind: “When she sees one sitting idle saying, ‘I have nothing,’ the good lady called Land will laugh,” Valluvar says beautifully in the chapter on agriculture.

Its meaning is this: seeing a man lament that he has no means to live, the maiden called Land seems to mock him and ask, “Why are you sitting idle and inactive? I am here. Are you unable to make use of me?” In the same way, there are abundant resources in Tamil Nadu. There is coal; there is iron; there are materials such as gypsum; there are sands suitable for atomic purposes, and so on. Why should these not be used? When these resources exist, why say that there are no facilities and that the schemes cannot be carried out?

**Hon. C. Subramaniam:** You seem to be intoxicated with power. What is referred to there are irrigation schemes.

<!-- source-page: 22 -->

**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** I do not become intoxicated, because I am an ardent supporter of prohibition.

Can these materials not be used here? Is it right to say there are no natural advantages? I wish to point out that, if it is said there are no natural advantages, that cannot be accepted.

The Honourable Member who spoke before me referred to Tuticorin harbour. If that harbour scheme is undertaken, it can become a deep-sea harbour capable of competing with Colombo harbour and Bombay harbour. If a canal of about one hundred miles is constructed, that harbour can be developed properly. If the rocks between the western coast of Ceylon and the eastern coast at our southern tip are removed, several more good harbours can be created. It is said that this would cost about ten crore rupees. North India is collecting about 35 crore rupees from the South by way of port taxes. I ask: can ten crore rupees out of that 35 crore not be taken and spent for the South? A committee headed by Mr. A. Ramasamy Mudaliar examined this Sethu Samudram scheme, submitted a report, and that report has been sent to the Central Government. That report too is still sleeping unattended. The North takes the taxes of our country and prospers, while we are made to remain enslaved to them. To increase the prosperity of our own land, we are made to depend on them. Ships that pass through the Suez Canal and come to other countries in the East, and ships that go to Colombo to obtain coal, if Tuticorin harbour—

<!-- source-page: 23 -->

—is enlarged, will come there and enrich the material wealth of the South. Why should we not undertake such a scheme? It can bring us profit and revenue. Through it, should we not raise the standard of living of our people? I ask why this Government should not place sound and productive schemes before the Central Government and fight to have them implemented. The taxes of Dravida Nadu are being poured and piled into the North. Why should we not at least obtain a little of that today and carry out these schemes? Because Dravida Nadu has not attained liberation, we have not risen in life. If we look at other countries in the world, the standard of living of their people has risen greatly. In Egypt, a person's average annual income is 465 rupees. In Lebanon, a person's average annual income is 600 rupees. In Switzerland, a person's average annual income is 4,000 rupees. In Iceland, a person's average annual income is even higher. But in our country alone, a person's average annual income is only 270 rupees. The standard of living of the people here has not risen at all. The Government is aware of all this. Yet we go on pouring out taxes in heaps upon heaps.

In the play Harischandra I have heard a song: “With the hand that once gave and gave to those who asked for silver — now I have had to receive for the funeral fire — I have become a wretched sinner.” It is said to have as its historical background the time when, in Yavana lands, Greece and Rome, trade goods were heaped up and Tamil children scattered gold coins in the streets and played with them—

<!-- source-page: 24 -->

Today we survive by receiving from the North for the funeral fire. In a cremation ground, the one who takes money for the funeral fire demands it in a commanding voice. Ours, however, is the voice of a slave. That is why the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam says: Deivanai of the South alone is enough; we do not want Valli of the North. But the Honourable Finance Minister says that Deivanai of the South alone is not enough; Valli of the North is also needed. Perhaps the reason is that our Honourable Finance Minister himself is Subramaniam.

In conclusion, I wish to state that it is our view that the golden day must come when separate Budgets are framed for Dravida Nadu and Dravida Nadu functions on its own. With the words, “May Dravidam triumph; long live Dravidam,” I conclude my speech.
