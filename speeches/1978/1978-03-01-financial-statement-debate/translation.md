**Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Mr Speaker, I wish to present my views, on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, on the financial statement for the year 1978-79.

Various members and party leaders who spoke here have pointed out that this House proceeded directly to the financial statement without the Governor's Address—the Government's policy document—being delivered, that this was not in keeping with the traditions of the House, and that it was deeply regrettable that such a lapse had occurred. They have also said that the members of this august House were thereby deprived of an opportunity to debate the Government's policy for six or seven days. I find myself in a position of endorsing those views.

When the debate arises as to whether there should be an office called Governor at all, the answer of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a different one. But so long as that office exists, abandoning this year the convention under which the Government's policy exposition is placed before the financial statement through the person holding that office is, in truth, not acceptable. I am duty-bound to point this out to the Leader of the House, my friend the Hon. Finance Minister.

Seven or eight months ago, when the financial statement was placed before this House, the size of the deficit was pointed out and certain methods were stated by which it would be made good; foremost among them, it was said, was that the deficit would be met by eliminating bribery, graft and corruption.
