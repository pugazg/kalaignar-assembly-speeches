## Finance Commission

Some Hon. members who spoke on this Financial Statement differed somewhat on an important point that I had underlined. In particular, Thiru Chowdi Sundarabharathi and some other members disagreed with my view that the Finance Commission should occupy the principal place in providing funds to the States. Thiru Chowdi Sundarabharathi said here that a principle had been put forward that funds should be allotted to the States through the Finance Commission and that he did not regard it as a correct principle.

My explanation to them is this: what I have stated in the Financial Statement is that the Finance Commission should occupy the principal place in creating the channel through which State Governments obtain finance from the Central Government. Our view is also that the Finance Commission itself should take Plan expenditure into account and make recommendations, and that the Planning Commission may be given responsibility for providing year-to-year assistance to the States for Plans in accordance with principles laid down by the Finance Commission. That is what I pointed out in this statement. It is not my argument that the Planning Commission should have no role whatever in allocating funds.

The State Government's share in Central taxes and the gap grants given by the Finance Commission are, under the present arrangement, provided in accordance with the recommendations of the Finance Commission. We say that this should continue. These are funds which the State Government has the right to spend according to its own choice. But, in the amount allotted by the Planning Commission towards State Plan expenditure—and I think it is good that everyone should understand this—

—70 per cent of the assistance given by the Planning Commission for State Plan expenditure is given as loan assistance and only 30 per cent as grant. What I say is that, as far as this grant assistance is concerned, the Planning Commission should give it to the States according to the general recommendations of the Finance Commission; this is also the view of the Central Administrative Reforms Commission.

As regards Plan loan assistance, this Government's view is that an all-India lending institution should be created to provide loans for large, specified Plan works, and that the States should also be represented in that institution.

Assistance for works carried out by the State on behalf of the Central Government, (Centrally sponsored schemes), and assistance for schemes initiated by the various departments of the Central Government and implemented in the State by the State Government—all these, we have said, should be brought under the recommendations of the Finance Commission. ## Concluding remarks

In this financial statement debate, I have spent a long time here giving my explanations on the many valuable points raised by the Hon. Leader of the Opposition and members of all parties.
