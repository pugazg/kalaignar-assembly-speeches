Speech 14 — Date: 10.03.1975

**Hon. Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Hon. Speaker, at the outset I convey my heartfelt thanks for the very fine arguments placed before this House by the leaders of all parties and by the Hon. Members on the financial statement laid before the House.

Among the many who took part in this debate and spoke, a few went deeply into the figures in the budget and said that the doubts that had arisen for them should be clarified and that certain errors had found a place in the financial statement.

When our friend Hande, who opened this debate, and our friend Edmund, seated before me in the Opposition benches, spoke, they referred to this as my final financial statement. In whatever sense it may be final, I wish only to assure my dear friends who call it “final” that the Kazhagam Government is carrying out its duty with a mind that is not troubled by that.

In regard to the budget, our Communist Party leader K.D.K. and our friend Hande raised some doubts. That is,

one charge was that capital expenditure had fallen below the previous year's revised estimate, and another charge, raised both by K.D.K. and Hande, was that the figure of Rs.41 crore this year was still lower.

When Mr Hande referred to this, he said:

> The budget bristles with a dangerous trend of systematic erosion into capital expenditure that may ultimately rain the development of our State.

Mr Thangamani too supported our friend Hande's view and said that this was the difference that had arisen in the capital expenditure provided for our development.

Our financial statement is divided into three sections. It is framed under three heads: the revenue account, the capital account (capital expenditure), and loans. This is the very method by which a budget is prepared.

If we take our 1975-76 financial statement as prepared in this manner, under the revenue account the total receipts are Rs.501.37 crore; total expenditure is Rs.537.50 crore; the deficit is Rs.36.13 crore.

In the revenue account, receipts are shown separately and expenditure separately. It was in that manner that I referred to this total account. The capital account is not like that. In the capital account, after taking total expenditure and total receipts, only the net expenditure—the net—is shown in the accounts. That is how we must calculate it. In the capital

### Source page 265

account, receipts are not shown separately. Only net expenditure is shown in the capital account. This has been the budget-accounting method for many years. In respect of capital expenditure, only after seeing what the total expenditure is and what the capital receipts are can we properly calculate whether capital expenditure has risen or not.

I wish to explain a little what is meant by capital expenditure. Construction of buildings—that is, office buildings, residential quarters, school buildings, college buildings, hospital buildings—besides buildings, irrigation schemes, construction of new roads, road-improvement schemes, share capital given to various Government undertakings, purchase of new buses for the Government transport department, purchase and stocking of agricultural inputs such as fertiliser for distribution to the people, forest development, and many such items are included in what is called net capital expenditure. Against capital expenditure, certain receipts also continue to come in every year.

In particular, the receipts obtained when fertilisers are sold are deducted from capital expenditure, and it is only what remains net after that which is customarily shown by us in the capital account.

For example, Dr Hande has referred to the final total in section 29 of the appendix to the financial statement. His point is that in the 1974-75 budget estimate capital expenditure was Rs.56.36 crore, whereas in the present estimate it has fallen to Rs.32.64 crore. My argument is that this is wrong.

In the 1974-75 budget estimate, total capital expenditure was Rs.86.83 crore. Where

### Source page 266

is this shown? If it is said that it is not mentioned here, those details are contained in the separately supplied book titled “Financial Note.”

Total capital expenditure was Rs.86.83 crore; the revised estimate for 1974-75 was Rs.99.71 crore. I have shown this in the supplementary grant. Of the Rs.86.83 crore shown as our capital expenditure in the 1974-75 budget estimate, receipts under that head amounted to Rs.30.47 crore. When that is deducted, the net expenditure is Rs.56.36 crore.

In the same way, in the present revised estimate of Rs.99.71 crore, receipts under that head amounting to Rs.67.06 crore have been deducted, and it is only in that manner that the net expenditure has come down to Rs.32 crore.

For this year, 1975-76, we have fixed total capital expenditure at Rs.103.80 crore. Receipts under this head are expected to be Rs.62.04 crore, leaving net capital expenditure of Rs.41.76 crore. Thus our total capital expenditure has been revised upward from Rs.86 crore to Rs.99 crore and has now been raised to Rs.103 crore. In the coming revised estimate it will only be higher; it has not fallen. I am duty-bound to remove their doubt on this point.

Total expenditure on the capital account, estimated at about Rs.87 crore in the 1974-75 financial year, grew to nearly Rs.100 crore in the revised estimate and has increased to Rs.104 crore in 1975-76. The revised estimate will be still higher. Therefore, through this explanation I wish to state that capital expenditure has in fact grown and has not fallen as others stated here.
