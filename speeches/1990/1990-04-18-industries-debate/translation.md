**Hon. Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Hon. Speaker, yesterday and today, members and leaders belonging to all parties have brought together and presented their views on the Industries grant and have told this Government the ways in which the industrial sector in Tamil Nadu can be developed further. From Subramanian, who began speaking yesterday on behalf of the Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, up to Kumari Ananthan, Deputy Leader of our Congress party, everyone who spoke has offered many constructive views on the development of industry in Tamil Nadu. Our friend Ugamchand alone kept looking around for some way or other to find fault with this Government and remained engaged in that effort until the end of his speech. I neither prevent nor condemn the effort he has taken to criticise this Government somehow; on the contrary, if even in that there are ways by which this Government should correct itself, I will accept them too, and I give the assurance that this Government will strive and work accordingly for industrial development.

Yesterday, our friend Poovaragan, while speaking, said that the amount allotted for this industrial development was very small.

He calculated only the amounts allotted under these two demands. The Hon. members of this House know very well that, rather than the State Government directly putting capital into industries, it is far more concerned with encouraging industries.

Therefore, even though the allocation made directly by the Government is of this order, if the amounts and capital made available through other financial institutions and banks and Government industrial undertakings, including equity, are all taken together, one can see that the allocation available for industry in our State is substantial. I spoke in this grant last year about this Government's industrial policy. I am duty-bound to remind the House of it again today.

Yesterday, our friend Ponnusamy, while speaking, placed before the House certain reports suggesting that we are today ruining all private industry, driving private entrepreneurs away, and that they are afraid even to start industries. Though I denied that allegation even then, as far as this Government is concerned, alongside the existing “Joint Sector,” last year we created another category called the “Associate Sector” and announced that such industries would be undertaken as joint ventures with the private sector, with the Government holding 20 per cent in the Joint Sector and 11 per cent in the Associate Sector. We have also already announced a scheme under which even these higher Government shareholdings may, at the appropriate time, be realised through “disinvestment” and the amount used for new industries.

Therefore, if 26 per cent is said to be the Government's share, nobody should imagine that “Government” is something situated somewhere apart from the people. The Government's 26 per cent is the people's share. Twenty-six per cent is the Government share; 25 per cent is the private sector's share; all the remaining percentage consists of the public's shares, their share capital. Thus the public's share capital and the Government's share capital are both, in essence, the people's share capital. Even if we separately call it “Government,” it is the people's share. In a situation where the people's share is so large, this Government will certainly not tolerate private parties who, without even holding the appropriate share capital and with a share smaller than that, nevertheless seek to exercise complete domination over an industry.
