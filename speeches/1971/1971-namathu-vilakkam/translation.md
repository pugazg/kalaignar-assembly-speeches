## Our Explanation

**Mr. Speaker!**

Members of this House have expressed their valuable views on the Budget Statement placed before the House. For expressing views both opposing and supporting the Budget Statement, I am duty-bound to thank them and welcome those views, in the belief that, in one way or another, they will be useful to the work of this Government.

It is not possible for me, in this general debate itself, to answer every single point that has been raised. Nor is there time for me to reply separately to each member's speech. Keeping these matters in view, from last year we devised a new method. In both Houses, when the grants relating to the various departments are taken up for discussion, arrangements are made for the overall views on the most important problems of those departments to be discussed there, and that practice is being implemented. I trust that when such departments are discussed here, the Ministers concerned will also give proper explanations.

I have not forgotten that many members who took part in the debate, even though their own consciences told them that this Budget Statement truly brought joy to their hearts, were compelled to oppose it because they were seated on the Opposition benches in this House.

Our honourable Ponnappa Nadar delivered his speech after giving the press the headline, “There is no revolution in this; there is no novelty in this!”

<!-- source-page: 5 -->

Whether there is revolution and novelty in this will become known only when the schemes indicated in this Statement are put into practice. I too did not imagine that, the moment I finished reading the Budget Statement, this great House would immediately turn upside down and become one great revolution; nor, I think, would the honourable Ponnappa Nadar have imagined so.

### The condition of those given chloroform

When many members were praising this Budget Statement, our Dr. Hande remarked: “What is there in this to praise? No one can praise it except those who have taken an anaesthetic—that is, those who have been given chloroform.” Those who have been given chloroform cannot even speak; good doctors know that!

For some reason, however, Dr. Hande described all those who praised the Budget as people under the anaesthetic effect of chloroform.

### Two views on taxation

Dr. Hande charged that this was a Budget Statement prepared without an understanding of economics. There is one view that imposing taxes will raise prices, and another view that it will not. Economic thinkers such as “Kinsmasgrace” have advanced the theory that, by reducing the money in people's hands through taxation, prices will fall. We must not forget that.

### The economics we know is to bring well-being to the poor and humble

As Dr. Hande says, I am no great economic scholar. Nor am I prepared to beat the drum and proclaim that I have prepared this Statement on the basis of such scholarly brilliance!

<!-- source-page: 6 -->

The economics that my party and I hold is simply this: the poor and humble, the ordinary middle-class working people and labouring people of this country must attain well-being; and we consider as our economics—the economics we particularly cherish—whatever we think through on the basis of what schemes must be drawn up to make that possible.

### Our Budget Statement in keeping with the hallmark of good government

Under the limit laid down today by the Central Government for this State Government, only Rs. 70 or 80 crore may be spent in a year through the Plan. But, as far as the Tamil Nadu Government is concerned, since our schemes cannot be confined within a limit of Rs. 70 or 80 crore, we have raised our one-year Plan from Rs. 100 crore to Rs. 105 crore. In keeping with the hallmark of good government—that the necessary sources of finance must be found—we examined every possible means and presented this Statement.

### The capital account—and making investments

Our friend Marimuthu expressed the view that, if only the revenue account were taken into account, taxes could be reduced accordingly. But only by taking the capital account together with it can we examine the total income and total expenditure, work out how the pay increase is to be adjusted, and make the investments needed for industrial growth under the capital account.

### The Budget Statement is for people's welfare, not to seek profit for the Government!

Under the heading of buildings and public works, we are in a position where various buildings and many other facilities have to be provided even where they bring no profit to the Government.

All these are for the convenience and welfare of the people. We have not prepared the Budget Statement with the aim that the Government itself should make a profit.

<!-- source-page: 7 -->

Therefore, we are in no position to escape from or hide from this aspect of capital expenditure. For such capital expenditure, we have to create a net surplus in the revenue account itself. I state this as an explanation to our friend Marimuthu's doubt.

It is because that surplus is not enough that we have to look at the total shortfall that falls across all the accounts taken together.

### Welcome schemes in the Budget

There are many schemes in this Budget that members welcome. Yet even some people among the allied parties—with the exception, in particular, of a few like Thiru Manali Kandasami—do not praise the schemes introduced for people's welfare. As far as I am concerned, that is a matter of regret.

Perhaps Thiru Manali Kandasami praised this Budget Statement because the Chief Minister is a man from Thanjavur.

When he asked, “If so many good things are to be done, what is wrong with increasing the sales tax by half a per cent?”, I felt greatly encouraged.

It gives me great pleasure that, in this tax measure, the Government has received the support of a good and capable man with such seasoned experience!

But I do not know what decision the members of the Communist Party of India took at the secret meeting they held among themselves after Manali spoke. They opposed this tax yesterday, and they have continued to speak against it today as well.

Our friend Thiru Arumugasami, speaking on behalf of the Praja Socialist Party on this Budget Statement, said that it “contained many full-fledged socialist measures

<!-- source-page: 8 -->

.” As I reply to the debate here, I am prepared to take him at his word.

I believe that no one of goodwill and genuine concern can deny that this Budget Statement has been prepared with the determination to bring that ideal into being.

### So that the poor may rise . . . . . .

In keeping with that aim, this Budget Statement contains many provisions that touch the poor and humble, our worker comrades, ordinary people and labouring people. For many long years, agricultural labourers have lived under the cruelty of being liable to eviction at any time from the house sites on which they live.

> **Source illustration note:** An editorial illustration with a map of Tamil Nadu points to the issue of agricultural workers' house sites; among the clearly legible words are references to Thanjavur district and agricultural residential house sites.

With the good intention of protecting them from that cruelty, we have drawn up a scheme to ensure that agricultural labourers are not evicted. Not only are they to be protected from eviction; this Government has accepted the decision that the house sites on which they live should become their own property.

We have planned to create this situation throughout the State. As a first step, in the soil of Thanjavur, I have today framed a scheme under which the house sites occupied by lakhs of agricultural labourers will be made their own, and we have announced it on behalf of the Government in this Budget Statement.

<!-- source-page: 9 -->

Today, lakhs of poor landless cultivators are without land pattas or house-site pattas. In keeping with what I have announced many times—that this Government has been providing them with land pattas and house-site pattas—we have laid down the principle that even lands called forests, but which are not truly forests, should be given to landless cultivators. The Minister of that department travelled to various districts in the State, examined the matter and returned; and, on that basis, we have now decided to assign up to 50,000 acres of such forest land to poor landless cultivators.

> **Source illustration note:** An editorial illustration shows two people holding what appears to be a model of land or a house site; unclear small lettering is not guessed.

Thiru K. T. K. Thangamani said that all this should be done by following Kerala.

### Innovations and revolutions in the lives of the poor!

The Communist Party first came to power in Kerala in 1957. From that year until 1971, for nearly thirteen or fourteen years—even though they were not continuously in office—those policies have been spread across Kerala, and the soil there has been prepared accordingly. We must not forget that.

But this Government seeks to bring about innovations and peaceful revolutions in the lives of the poor and humble on a scale that does not yield even a little to Kerala; it works so that the poor may walk with their heads held high and that smiles may appear on their faces. Members know very well how many years this Government has been in office, and how many storms of sorrow the ruling party has had to withstand in the meantime.

<!-- source-page: 10 -->

### Nationalisation of buses

You have welcomed the nationalisation of passenger buses; you have also welcomed the imposition of an upper limit on them.

On the nationalisation of passenger buses—on bringing them into public ownership—the Leader of the Opposition and other members with progressive views expressed their opinions.

Friends such as Swaminathan, and people such as our Swatantra Party leader Thiru Ganesan, asked: “You are unable to run properly even the bus administration you are operating now. How are you going to take this over too and run it?”

Those who asked this fell into two groups: some asked because they wanted public ownership; others asked on the basis that you cannot run it and therefore this policy itself is unnecessary.

I do not wish to separate them out and say who belongs to which group. I gave an explanation even then. The Government is incurring a loss on buses running in Madras city. On the buses running outside—passenger buses connecting many towns—we are making a profit. The profits from the buses running outside are sufficient, to some extent, to offset the loss incurred here.

Since all the buses we are now nationalising are buses running outside the city, and since we need not operate each of them as a separate administration, there is no reason for a loss to arise.

Please do not misunderstand me as simply rejecting what you have said about shortcomings in the administration. This Government is working with the intention of further improving that administration. We are engaged in the necessary measures.

<!-- source-page: 11 -->

When our friend Swaminathan spoke, he said: “Even without paying the interest due to the Government, you are incurring a loss of nearly Rs. 1½ crore. If the interest payable to the Government is also included, the loss comes to about Rs. 2½ crore.”

### Loss: an explanation!

I wish to give him an explanation. The figure shown as a loss is the loss after the amount of interest payable to the Government has been paid; it is nothing other than that.

**Thiru K. Rajaram:** But this transport department does not pay income tax to the Government. Privately run bus companies pay income tax. The Government must accept this.

> **Source illustration note:** In an editorial illustration resembling an open book, the words “Tamil Nadu Chief Minister's Budget Statement” are clearly visible; the smaller writing on a blackboard-like panel is not clear and is not guessed.

**Chief Minister:** If there is income, they will pay tax. Since there is no income, income tax cannot be paid. But all the other taxes are being paid.

Therefore, the loss being referred to is only the loss that remains after the amount paid to the Government towards interest is taken out; it would not be correct to think that a loss occurred without even paying the interest.

Experimental studies are now being conducted in some depots to examine and investigate what the defects in the administration are and the various reasons for these breakdowns.

These will be accelerated rapidly, and we will take every effort within our power to put the Tamil Nadu Government transport department in order.

<!-- source-page: 12 -->

### Employment opportunities

You, as members of this House, also welcome the fact that we have made provision for employment.

One thousand people with S.S.L.C. qualifications will be directly recruited to the Police Department. Loan assistance will be provided to 3,000 engineers to start small industries. Employment will be provided to 2,000 graduates through the Youth Corps. In rural areas, we are arguing that the amount the State Government receives from the Central Government to provide employment to 15,000 people is insufficient. Without waiting until the outcome of that argument is known, through our State Government we are providing employment to another 15,000 people. Thus, we are providing employment to 30,000 people in the rural areas. In addition, we are providing employment to 150 doctors and 200 nurses. Situations of this kind are being created today.

### Selection only for those with merit and ability

We will not forget what members on the Opposition side and in the allied parties have said—that there must be no party bias in this. Those who are qualified and capable for these posts alone will be selected. They will not be selected merely on the ground that this person belongs to the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam or that this person knows that person and therefore must be chosen. Selection will be made without party bias.

Whoever has merit and ability—whatever party they may until then have belonged to, whatever party feeling may exist in their hearts—we will not concern ourselves with any of that. Those who are capable, those who have the ability to carry out the duties of this Government properly, will be selected. That is certain!

Employment and the hardship of unemployment are of the greatest importance. Today this problem lies spread across the whole expanse of India; we cannot forget that.

<!-- source-page: 13 -->

When the Leader of the Opposition spoke here, he said, with good intent and sound thought: “I welcome the schemes this Government has drawn up for employment. But for an elephant's hunger this is like popped maize. Therefore this employment programme must be expanded.”

I accept that. But I know that friends who wish to use this for propaganda will seize on it as a reason and put in the newspapers that Thiru Rajaram Naidu said the employment programme was like popped maize for an elephant's hunger. I know, too, that this scheme goes only that far. Even so, he has at least acknowledged that this Government has recognised the situation; for that I thank him.
