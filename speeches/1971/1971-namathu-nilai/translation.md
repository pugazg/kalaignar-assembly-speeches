# English translation

## Editorial Unit 1 — Reply to the debate on the Motion of Thanks for the Governor's Address

<!-- source-page: 3 -->

# Our Position

In the joint sitting consisting of members of the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and Legislative Council, Governor Sardar Ujjal Singh delivered his address on 24-3-1971. Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi replied in both Houses to the debate on the Motion of Thanks for that address. His speech follows:-

With reference to the Motion of Thanks on the Governor's Address moved in this House by member Thiru Vezhavendan, the leaders and members of all parties have taken part and expressed their views.

A total of 67 members, including Thiru Ponnappa Nadar, leader of the Nijalingappa Congress Party, who spoke last, participated in this debate and delivered speeches.

Although there are 183 members here on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, 33 members spoke on behalf of the Kazhagam. Although the Opposition has a total of 52 members, 34 of them were given an opportunity to speak here.

This fact alone is clear proof that the ruling party treats those on the Opposition side with due respect.

When members spoke about the election referred to in the Governor's Address, the debate became heated.

## Unity in opposing evil

But from the fact that each member described the untoward incidents that had occurred in his constituency and then said that care should be taken to ensure that such incidents do not recur, it is very clear that, as far as Tamil Nadu is concerned, no one wants these violent rampages.

<!-- source-page: 4 -->

When Muslim League leader Tiruppur Moideen spoke, he put it beautifully: "Let us forget what has happened, accept the democratic verdict, and all work together to make the country's future bright."

At a time when we think we should work in that way, propaganda that the election just concluded was somehow fraudulent or had been conducted wrongly — whether it is made here in the House or outside — is neither good nor appropriate.

As soon as the election result was announced, a statement made by Masani, the senior leader of the Swatantra Party, was published.

## Democratic tradition

The newspapers carried remarks by Swatantra Party leader Thiru Masani saying that, as a democrat, he accepted the votes which the voters had given with confidence to Parliament and to his party as the verdict delivered by the people. That is a good democratic tradition worthy of praise and appreciation.

Next, when member Ponnappa Nadar spoke, he said that there had been some chemical mixture in the ink, that some tricks had taken place, that acts of magic had occurred.

I understand what he means when he refers to acts of magic. Even today it is being said and written that "there is something wrong with the ink," that "once it is applied it dries immediately," and that "it changes into something else."

## Tata's denial

When a question was raised as to whether there had been fraud in the ballot paper, Tata, who had contested as an Independent with the support of some parties in the Democratic Alliance, issued a statement denying that allegation.

<!-- source-page: 5 -->

Naval Tata issued a statement denying the report that he intended to file a case challenging his defeat. The views he expressed in that statement were as follows:

"I do not wish to file any petition challenging the election result. I never even thought of doing so. The report that I am going to litigate is mischievous. The allegations that chemical ink was used on the ballot papers are far-fetched; imaginary. If the allegations that mysterious chemical ink was used and that there were special ballot papers were true, I would have polled only about 20,000 votes. I have become like a fish taken out of water. I want to go back into the water."

I am citing all this only to show how magnanimously they accepted the verdict of the people.

## A clear verdict

Similarly, Rajya Sabha member Thiru R. D. Parthasarathi has said: "Because the people gave overwhelming support to the Indira Congress in the recently concluded election, it has been proved that the people expressed their faith in its progressive policy. The people gave overwhelming support to Indira Gandhi's leadership. As a democrat, I accept the people's verdict." He has since even left that Congress. A few others have gone with him as well.

<!-- source-page: 6 -->

Thus, they accepted the election result with great magnanimity.

## A flaw is one thing; an offence is another.

Many people here have said that still more modern methods should be used in elections. I have no differing view on that. More modern methods are indeed needed.

But while there may be a few shortcomings here and there in an election, it is not good for us to say that there were offences. We must think deeply about the difference between shortcomings and offences.

Shortcomings are things that must be found and removed immediately wherever they occur. Acts knowingly committed in violation are offences. No such offence took place in this election.

When our highly respected member Manali Kandasami spoke, he pointed out that caste feelings had raised their heads in this election; that gods had been dragged into it; and that theist-atheist arguments had been used unnecessarily in the election.

Yes! In this election we saw communal feelings being stirred up to an extent never seen before. But we also saw that those communal forces did not win on a majority scale.

<!-- source-page: 7 -->

An incident that took place in Salem city was linked to the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam and linked to the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam Government, and it was argued that therefore people should not vote for the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. In this way they tied the election itself to the Salem incident.

## The Salem incident — an explanation!

I say even today: no one approved of the Salem incident. No one can accept what happened in Salem.

Two or three days after the Salem incident, when I had gone to Madurai and reporters met me and asked about it, what I said was: "I do not accept the Salem incidents; the police will take action regarding them." I said that an explanation had been sought from them; I did not ask, "What was wrong in the Salem incident?"

But even after the Salem incident was over, was it necessary to make huge posters depicting everything that had happened there and paste them all over the town? We must think about that. If they truly had faith in God, would they do such a thing?

What had they put on the posters? They showed pictures of Raman and Murugan, showed Periyar as though he were striking them with a slipper, and showed me standing there watching and laughing. Those posters were pasted all over the town.

We must consider whether that is truly something that can be permitted. If the Salem incident was wrong, should posters be pasted all over the town to show that this was how the wrong had taken place?

<!-- source-page: 8 -->

## Is it right to demonstrate a wrong by repeating it?

A man strikes another man's father during a quarrel. At the time he is being struck, someone also takes a photograph of it. The son of the man who was struck will go and file a case against the man who struck him. Or he may simply leave the matter there rather than take it to court and make the dispute so large.

Instead of either of these, suppose that, in order to tell the world that the man who struck him was wicked, he enlarges the photograph, makes a printing block from it, prints attractive colour posters and pastes them all over the town. What would happen? Would that father not call his son and say in distress, "You sinner! He struck me only in one place; you have made me be struck all over the town"?

## A verdict in which Mahesan too joined

Our Perarignar Anna used to say often, "The people's verdict is Mahesan's verdict." We must not forget that this verdict is one in which Mahesan too joined.

Thiru Ponnappa Nadar remarked that under this Government all freedom of the press had been taken away.

Some action was taken because certain hateful, undesirable pictures — pictures unfit for publication — were published in a newspaper. I do not wish to go into the merits of those actions.

During the four years of this Government, against how many newspapers did it take action? From how many newspapers did it demand a security deposit? How many plays did it ban? How many books did it ban?

<!-- source-page: 9 -->

## Freedom of the press: then and now.

Do you know how many newspapers were proceeded against during twenty years of Congress rule? During the anti-Hindi agitation of 1965, was action not taken against the newspaper `Mithiran`? Was its editor not arrested? Was action not taken against `Maalai Mani`? Did they not enter its office and arrest the editor?

Were newspapers such as `Viduthalai` and `Dravida Nadu` not asked to furnish security deposits?

Was the play `Iraniyan` not banned? Were the plays `Thookkumedai` and `Udhaya Sooriyan` not banned? Was the play `Porvaal` not banned?

As for books, were not many books such as `Periyar Pon Mozhi`, `Ilatchiya Varalaru` and `Ravana Kaviyam` prohibited?

How can they say that the Government issued prohibitory orders and that freedom of the press has been taken away? When something is written with an evil intention so that people should personally misunderstand someone in our Cabinet or anyone in the ruling party, where else are we to take the matter if not to court?

The newspapers published a report saying that Karunanidhi had used his party's money — the election fund — to make a film. At every meeting I said, "No, no." They kept saying, "Yes, yes." We went to court because only by going to court could a proper resolution be found.

When those who go to court say they are prepared to accept the judgment delivered by the court, those who wrote the allegations must also be prepared to accept that judgment.

<!-- source-page: 10 -->

## Who spoke of banning a play?

All that was done was to take action to deny the slanders levelled against us by some newspapers and establish the truth. No newspaper was banned. No play was banned. Even when, at a time when the Congress was still united, Congress members spoke in favour of banning a particular play, I rose and said, "It is not good to take away the right to write. As far as we are concerned, let the people support us if they wish. If not, let them go without supporting us!" I did not say, "Yes, the play must be banned." The country knows this well. It was also reported in the newspapers.

## Politics and murders.

Thiru Ponnappa Nadar stated that 17 murders had occurred during the election period. If we say that 17 murders took place, they were incidents that occurred before and after the election period. It cannot be said that all the murders that occurred were connected with the election. It would be wrong to say so.

Of the 17 murders, nine or ten were politically connected. Our information is that the others arose from personal disputes among those concerned.

Since all those cases are before the courts, I have not gone into them in greater detail. Even so, murders must not take place. Violent incidents must not occur. Yet while we are speaking about this every day in this House, even two days ago, in the Parangimalai constituency — the constituency from which M.G.R. was elected — Murugesan, who belonged to the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, was stabbed to death.
