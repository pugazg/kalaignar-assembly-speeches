**Speech: 8**  
**Date: 29.04.1999**

**Hon. Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi:** Hon. Speaker, many Hon. Legislative Assembly members, speaking on the demand for grants for Industries, have set out their thoughtful and substantial views, appreciating what is good and compiling the needs that must be met. First, Thiru Ranganathan, member of the Tamil Maanila Congress party, who opened this debate, appreciated the rapid progress that has taken place in industrial development in Tamil Nadu today, while at the same time strongly insisting that all the factories that are closed must be reopened. The answer obtained when one asks whether those factories were closed because of disputes among workers or because of the Government’s functioning is: “No.” Many explanations have been given in this House on behalf of the Government that those factories have been closed because of the management ability of those industrialists, shortage of finance, disputes among them, and the situation in which matters concerning them are pending before the courts. Factories lying closed is not news that gives the Government any happiness. I inform our friend Ranganathan that the Government will pay attention to how they may be reopened and what efforts may be undertaken.

By starting industries in the southern districts, bringing about good economic development there, and putting an end to the caste conflicts that are now emerging—

### Source page 242

—that good suggestion, which is also a view the Government already holds, was expressed by Sivaraman, who spoke here on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. I have stated in the Budget that a committee will be constituted to give recommendations on how this Government may bring about industrial growth, people’s advancement and various other improvements in the southern districts. Therefore, I also inform the House that this will be done soon and that the committee will be announced.

Our Subbarayan, who spoke on behalf of the Communist Party of India, pointed to the severe problem now facing the textile-mill industry and also referred to the fact that I called both sides and held a long discussion with them here in the Fort, in the Namakkal Kavignar Maaligai. We have repeatedly explained that this is not a State-level problem but an all-India problem, that the Central Government must find a solution at the all-India level, and, stressing this, we have made many requests to the Central Government on behalf of the State Government.

While speaking, he referred to one matter — ‘TANCEM’. In speaking about it, he sharply criticised the Industries Secretary. For Subbarayan, criticising officials is like eating athirasam. (Laughter.) But I wish to say one thing. I am duty-bound to place on record with gratitude that, among those responsible for industrial growth in Tamil Nadu having reached this level, both the Industries Secretary who was in office when we assumed responsibility for the Government and the Industries Secretary who is in office now are important contributors. (Sound of desk-thumping.)

When he referred to ‘TANCEM’, he also mentioned the Mayanur asbestos factory. On that, I wish to give a brief explanation.

### Source page 243

Under the Government Order issued on 19-2-1997, in accordance with the Finance Department’s order, the purchase protection and price preference that had been given to Government undertakings were withdrawn. Until 30-3-1998, TWAD had been obtaining its entire requirement of pipes and fittings from TANCEM itself. Therefore, after this Government Order was issued, TWAD proceeded to obtain its asbestos-pipe requirements through the general tender method. To meet its requirements for 1998-99, TWAD took steps to procure pipes worth Rs. 22 crore through the general tender method. The L-1 tenderer in this was M/s. Infrastructure, Rajasthan. Its price was Rs. 11,964 per Metric Tonne.

According to its requirements, TWAD asked TANCEM to agree to the L-1 price quoted in the tender. TANCEM agreed to supply at the L-1 price only to the extent of 50 per cent, and it received that share, which I wish to convey. Therefore, TANCEM has not been completely ignored. I inform him that 50 per cent has been given to it.

Next, when Thiru Subbarayan spoke, he referred to the difficulty that has arisen in Tiruppur in relation to infrastructure and development. The extent to which he, I, and the Minister for Local Administration have taken concern and worked on this, and how we are to face the present interim stay of the court and come out of it, are matters now under consideration. Therefore, I inform Thiru Subbarayan that we shall soon succeed in our effort to begin that development project.

Thiru Sundaram, who spoke on behalf of the AIADMK, asked: “You went to Singapore; whom did you see, and what did you bring back?” Although Thiru Kuzhanthai Tamilarasan gave some explanations in reply, I met the Prime Minister in Singapore; I met the Ministers; I met Singapore industrialists. Even now, a large group of 20 members headed by Thiru Dato Samivel, a Public Works Minister, has come to Tamil Nadu and has conveyed to us its confidence that the Colachel port in Kanyakumari district can be improved, changing the present situation in which that already functioning port has ceased to operate, and that, although it is more or less a small port, it can be developed after the Tuticorin port even to a comparable level. A discussion on this lasting about two hours took place in this very Fort a few days ago. Likewise, with their full effort — this is not an agreement entered into by private individuals; it is an agreement between the Malaysian Government and the Tamil Nadu Government. Next, I say “flying road” only so that it will be understood. A plan has been prepared to construct, as a first stage, a two-tier road from the port up to Meenambakkam airport, and the designs for it were also discussed that day. Therefore, the visit to Malaysia and Singapore was not a matter of taking a Government aircraft at Government expense—

Thiru Shanmugam, who spoke on behalf of the DMK, said that Pollachi should be declared an industrially backward taluk. I have explained in this House, not once but many times, both during Question Hour and on several other occasions, what conditions and what kinds of criteria are required for an area to be declared a backward taluk. I inform him that, when the requisite criteria are met, consideration will be given to declaring Pollachi a backward taluk.

Mohamed Ismail, who spoke on behalf of the Indian National League, said that a drumstick factory was needed in his constituency. I assure him that it can be established and Bhagyaraj can be invited to conduct the inauguration. (Laughter.)

Our Ismail also spoke here about the extraction of sand from the Kudaganar and the shortcomings in it. Thiru Iyer, who is now the Karur District Collector, has uncovered in a major way the corrupt practices taking place in this sand quarry and is taking appropriate action. I inform Mohamed Ismail that orders will similarly be issued to take appropriate action in this district.

Dr. Chellakumar, who spoke on behalf of the Tamil Maanila Congress party, made an allegation. He asked why the file relating to the Urban Land Ceiling of the Best and Crompton company had been sleeping for ten months and what mystery lay hidden in it. “What is the mystery in watching from concealment?” is a song I heard in *Thillana Mohanambal*. Younger brother Chellakumar too would have heard it and watched it with interest. Therefore, there is no mystery in this. Merely because a file remains pending for some days, one should not conclude that there is a mystery in it.

The issue here is that, over and above the agreement they had already entered into, they sought permission to sell the surplus vacant land there. An officer or a Minister cannot simply issue an order for that. Even the Chief Minister cannot issue such an order. If it is very necessary and conducive to the public interest, the Cabinet must be convened and the decision taken there. That is how it is in all such matters.

He himself knows this. C. Subramaniam, who is the chairman of the trust board, has written to me about constructing a 22-storey building on the Congress grounds. Even that has taken ten months. Did he ask me, “Ten months have passed; what mystery is there in between?” C.S. would not suspect me in that way, and I too would not conduct myself in a manner deserving such suspicion; everyone, including the Leader of the Opposition belonging to the Tamil Maanila Congress party, knows that very well. Nevertheless, to grant that permission, we convened the Cabinet. I did not simply put my signature on it myself and say that permission had been granted. When a new policy position is to be taken, that position has to be taken in a Cabinet meeting.

Likewise, the Best & Crompton request is merely awaiting discussion and decision at a Cabinet meeting; there is nothing else behind it. It is surplus vacant land. All of these have now joined together and are functioning as a consortium under the name Siemens. All of them together are Siemens. It has been given to that entity and not to anyone else. A detailed reply has been sent to the letter written by Union Minister Valappadi Ramamurthi, and he too has accepted it. I inform Thiru Chellakumar that the political discussions arising in between have no connection whatsoever with this matter.
