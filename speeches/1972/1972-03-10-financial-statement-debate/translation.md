On the basis of the views they have offered, the various questions they have raised and the doubts they have expressed, I am duty-bound to give my reply in this House to the extent possible.

When the leader of our Communist Party, Mr K.T.K. Thangamani, spoke here, if one closed one's eyes for a moment, one could hardly avoid the feeling that we were all sitting in the opposition benches of the Kerala Legislative Assembly and that Kerala's Finance Minister was giving us a list of all the achievements made there. To that extent, forgetting Tamil Nadu entirely, he set out all the good things that the coalition government of his party in Kerala had done.

I do not wish to quarrel with the State of Kerala and claim that this State has implemented all manner of progressive schemes in competition with it. If Kerala has, as he said, advanced to that degree of progress, there is nothing wrong in our acquiring the feeling that we too should move towards equally just and progressive conditions.
